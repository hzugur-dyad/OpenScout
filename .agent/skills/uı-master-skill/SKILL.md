\# UI/UX Design Master Skill



Follow these principles to ensure all designs are polished, professional, and user-centric, avoiding common "vibe-coded" AI mistakes.



\## 1. Layout, Spacing \& Grid System

\- \*\*Grid Discipline:\*\* Always use a 3-column or 2-column grid for mobile/dashboard layouts. 

\- \*\*Auto Layout:\*\* Utilize Auto Layout consistently to maintain pixel-perfect vertical and horizontal spacing. Ensure elements have room to breathe; increase spacing for mobile screens.

\- \*\*Desktop Standard:\*\* For desktop apps, follow the "Apple Standard": Top bar for global actions (with space for window dragging), sidebar for navigation, and central main content.

\- \*\*Anti-AI Layouts:\*\* Avoid AI's tendency for overly busy or repetitive KPI cards. Collapse complex buttons into triple-dot menus or popovers to reduce visual noise.



\## 2. Visual Hierarchy \& Effects

\- \*\*Shadows \& Gradients:\*\* Avoid harsh Figma default shadows. Use subtle, light gray shadows with high blur or no shadow at all. Never use blue-green gradients; stick to variations of the same color for a cleaner look.

\- \*\*Context Over Clutter:\*\* Add helpful context-rich elements (e.g., small icons or realistic imagery) in empty spaces rather than leaving them "sterile".

\- \*\*Consistency:\*\* Use consistent corner radiuses (e.g., 10px for smaller components) across all buttons, cards, and input fields.

\- \*\*Progressive Disclosure:\*\* Hide advanced filters or data until the user specifically needs them (e.g., empty states that invite action).



\## 3. Colors \& Typography

\- \*\*The 4-Layer Foundation:\*\* Establish 4 layers of backgrounds, 2 stroke variants, and 3 text variants (Primary: 11-15% white, Subtext: 30-40% white).

\- \*\*Dark Mode Strategy:\*\* Dark mode is not just an inversion. Double the distance between color shades (4-6% white difference) to maintain distinction.

\- \*\*Professional Accents:\*\* Don't let AI choose bright, clashing colors. Use OKLCH palettes for perceived brightness consistency across charts and semantic colors (Success: Green, Error: Red).

\- \*\*Borders:\*\* Instead of black borders, use ~85% white strokes on light mode cards to define edges subtly.



\## 4. Navigation \& Interaction

\- \*\*Icons:\*\* Replace emojis with professional icon libraries like Phosphor or Lucide. Ensure icons are consistent in stroke weight and style.

\- \*\*Interactive Feedback:\*\* Every action needs immediate feedback. Use button state changes (gray out on click), loading wheels, or notification dots (e.g., a red dot on a 'Saved' tab).

\- \*\*Mobile Swipes:\*\* Use springy/bouncing easing curves for natural motion, not linear. Design fluid transitions where content follows the swipe direction.

\- \*\*User Flow:\*\* Always include "Skip" buttons, search bars in lists, and clear navigation links to prevent dead-ends.



\## 5. Copy \& Personality

\- \*\*Natural Language:\*\* Avoid "corporate AI mush." Use friendly, human-centric text (e.g., "We sweat the details" instead of "Attention to detail is our priority").

\- \*\*Delightful 404s:\*\* Use 404 pages as an opportunity to be quirky, fun, or interactive to turn a negative experience into a positive one.



\## 6. Charts \& Data Presentation

\- \*\*Simplicity Over Aesthetic:\*\* Prioritize readability. Avoid rounded bars or missing axes in charts. Use shaded maps or rich data visualizations instead of boring bar charts where possible.



\## 7. Product Presentation

\- \*\*Mockups:\*\* When presenting designs, use high-quality Apple product mockups. Use subtle glows or glassy backgrounds to make the UI "pop".
